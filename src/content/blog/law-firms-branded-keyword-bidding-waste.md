# The SEO Trap of Branded Keyword Bidding (When Law Firms Waste Money on Their Own Name)

Should your law firm bid on its own name in Google Ads? Short answer: probably not, unless a competitor is bidding on it first or your organic presence is broken. Is branded keyword PPC worth it if you already rank organically for your firm name? Almost never—at least not at the budget allocations most firms are running. The math on **branded keywords law firm PPC** is surprisingly simple, and once you run it, you'll wonder how it ever made it into your media plan.

Here's the problem: when someone searches your firm name, they already know who you are. That's not top-of-funnel behavior. That's someone who has already decided to look you up. If you're ranking in position one organically—and you should be for your own name—you're now paying Google to intercept your own referral traffic. You're not acquiring a new client. You're taxing a conversion that was already going to happen.

---

## The Math Most Firms Never Run

Let's walk through the logic without inventing numbers that don't apply to your market.

You rank organically at position one for your firm name. Organic click-through rates at position one typically run between 25% and 40% of impressions for branded queries, often higher because the searcher is specifically looking for you. Now you add a branded PPC ad on top of that. Some percentage of those clicks shift from the free organic result to your paid ad. You've just converted a zero-cost click into a paid one.

The only scenario where that math improves: **if the paid ad captures incremental clicks that your organic listing would have missed entirely**. That can happen. But for most law firms running branded campaigns, the real-world lift in total clicks is marginal—and the cost is not.

## When Branded Bidding Actually Makes Sense

There are real scenarios where bidding on **branded keywords law firm PPC** is justified. Here are the ones worth taking seriously:

**1. A competitor is actively bidding on your name.**

This is the most legitimate reason. If a rival firm is appearing above your organic result by bidding on your firm name, you have a problem. Searchers who intended to find you can be intercepted—especially on mobile, where the fold is shorter and ads dominate the visible screen. In this case, bidding defensively is often worth the cost, and the CPCs are usually lower because you have higher quality scores on your own brand terms.

To know whether this is happening, you need to be running [competitive intelligence on your top competitors' marketing activity](/blog/law-firm-competitor-marketing-spend-analysis/) on a regular basis—not just checking once a year.

**2. Your organic presence for your own name is actually broken.**

This happens more than firms want to admit. A site migration gone wrong, a penalty, a knowledge panel that's pointing somewhere else, a recently rebranded firm that hasn't rebuilt authority under the new name. If users searching your firm name aren't finding you organically in the first two or three results, paid coverage makes sense while you fix the underlying problem. It is not a long-term solution.

**3. You're running a specific campaign you need to control.**

If you're promoting a specific landing page—a seminar, a new practice area launch, a targeted intake funnel—branded ads can direct high-intent name-searchers to a specific destination rather than your homepage. Even then, evaluate whether a sitelink extension on your organic result accomplishes the same thing at zero cost. Often it does.

## The Practice Areas Where This Hurts Most

Branded keyword waste is not evenly distributed across firm types. It tends to hit hardest in competitive personal injury, criminal defense, and family law markets—exactly the practices where firms are already [spending heavily on non-branded PPC](/blog/google-local-service-ads-law-firms/) and margin pressure is real.

In those markets, branded CPCs can run surprisingly high because competitors have driven up the floor on anything tangentially related to a firm's name. You end up in a bidding war for your own brand equity—brand equity you built, that your competitor is now monetizing by making you pay to defend it.

This is also where **branded keywords law firm PPC** spending tends to go unexamined the longest. It gets bucketed into the total PPC report, performance looks fine in aggregate because branded terms convert well (of course they do—the person was already looking for you), and nobody pulls it out to question whether those conversions were actually incremental.

---

## What the Reporting Usually Hides

Here's where it gets uncomfortable. Most Google Ads accounts for law firms don't segment branded from non-branded performance. Everything goes into one campaign or one reporting view, and the average CPA looks reasonable because branded conversions—which are easy and cheap to attribute—are averaging down the non-branded CPAs that are actually doing the heavy lifting of client acquisition.

When you separate them, you often find that your branded campaign has a CPA that looks fantastic and a non-branded campaign that looks alarming. The solution most firms reach for is to cut the alarming campaign. The correct solution is to recognize that the branded campaign's CPA is largely illusory—those conversions were mostly going to happen anyway.

This is the same analytical blind spot that affects [how law firms allocate budget between brand and performance marketing more broadly](/blog/law-firm-brand-performance-marketing-budget-allocation/). When attribution is muddled, brand spend looks like performance spend, and firms keep funding it long after it stops earning its place.

## Running the Audit Yourself

If you're managing your own Google Ads account, here's the minimum you need to do:

- **Segment your campaigns by brand vs. non-brand.** If they're mixed, separate them. This is non-negotiable for clean analysis.
- **Pull a search terms report** on your branded campaigns. See exactly what's triggering those ads. You may find your firm name, slight variations, and—occasionally—completely unrelated queries that have crept in.
- **Compare conversion volume before and after pausing** branded campaigns. Run a test. Pause branded for two weeks, watch what happens to total organic conversions. In most cases, the drop is far smaller than the branded campaign's reported conversion volume suggests.
- **Check competitor activity on your brand terms** using the Auction Insights report. If no competitors are showing up there, your defensive bidding argument evaporates.

## The Bottom Line

Bidding on your own firm name is one of the most widespread and least scrutinized budget leaks in law firm digital marketing. It feels safe because the performance metrics look good. It feels strategic because it's protecting your brand. In most cases, it's neither—it's just paying a toll to Google on traffic that was already yours.

There are real exceptions. Defend your brand when competitors are actively targeting it. Use paid branded coverage as a bridge when your organic presence is genuinely compromised. Control the destination when you have a specific conversion objective that justifies it. Outside of those scenarios, that budget almost always works harder somewhere else.
