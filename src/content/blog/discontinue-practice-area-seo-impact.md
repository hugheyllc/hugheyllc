# The Practice Area Sunset: How to Wind Down a Failing Practice Line Without Tanking Your SEO

Removing a practice area from your law firm website is straightforward. You delete the pages, take the navigation link down, and move on. What is not straightforward is what happens to your SEO when you do that carelessly. **Retiring a practice area** without a plan leaves crawl errors, dead internal links, lost backlink equity, and a domain authority profile that quietly erodes over the next six months. The short answer to whether you should keep old practice area pages if you no longer practice in that area: no, but you need to handle the exit correctly or you will pay for it in rankings across your entire site, not just on the pages you removed.

This post walks through exactly how to sunset a practice line without turning your domain into a 404 wasteland.

---

## Why Killing a Practice Area Carelessly Hurts Your Whole Domain

Most firms treat a practice area removal like deleting a folder. The associate left, the work dried up, the economics stopped making sense — so someone goes into the CMS and hits delete on thirty pages. Done.

Except it is not done. Those pages had inbound links from directories, bar association listings, guest posts, and partner websites. They had internal links from your homepage, your attorney bios, your blog archive. Google had crawled and indexed them. Some of them ranked for terms that, while not your core business anymore, still drove brand-adjacent traffic.

When you delete them without redirects, every one of those signals hits a dead end. Google registers the 404s, devalues the linking pages slightly, and flags your site as one that does not maintain itself well. Do this across thirty pages and you have a real crawl health problem. **Retiring a practice area law firm SEO** cleanup is not glamorous work, but it directly protects the rankings you actually care about.

---

## Step One: Audit Before You Touch Anything

Before you delete a single page, pull three reports:

1. **Backlink inventory** — Use whatever tool your SEO vendor uses (Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz) and export every external link pointing to the practice area pages you are removing. Note the domain authority of the linking sites. High-authority links pointing to pages you are about to delete represent equity you can salvage.

2. **Internal link map** — Crawl your own site and identify every internal link that points to the practice area pages. Your blog posts, your attorney bio pages, your related practice area sidebars — they all need to be updated or redirected. If you have ever stumbled into [the sidebar trap of linking related practice areas everywhere](/blog/law-firm-sidebar-related-practice-areas/), now is when that habit costs you extra cleanup time.

3. **Organic traffic history** — Pull twelve months of traffic data for the pages you are sunsetting. Some of them may be pulling more traffic than you realized, even if they are not converting. This affects whether you redirect, consolidate, or simply let certain pages downgrade gracefully.

---

## Step Two: Decide What Gets Redirected, Consolidated, or Retired

Not every page in a retiring practice line deserves the same treatment. Here is a simple framework:

**Redirect to a related active practice area** when the content is topically adjacent. If you are dropping elder law but keeping estate planning, redirect elder law pages to your estate planning hub. The topical relevance is close enough that Google treats this as a reasonable signal transfer rather than a dead end.

**Consolidate into a single summary page** when you have a cluster of thinly related pages that collectively built authority but individually said very little. This is a common problem — firms end up with [dozens of pages covering the same topic from slightly different angles](/blog/content-fragmentation-law-firm-websites/) because someone was trying to hit keyword variations. Combine them into one well-structured page before you redirect or remove anything.

**Retire without redirect** only when the content has zero topical overlap with anything you currently practice and has minimal backlinks. In this case, a 410 (Gone) response is cleaner than a redirect to an irrelevant page. A bad redirect — sending a personal injury page to your corporate transactional practice — does not transfer authority. It confuses Google and irritates anyone who lands there.

---

## Step Three: Salvage the Authority Before It Walks Out the Door

This is the step most firms skip because it requires actual outreach, and nobody wants to do outreach.

For every high-authority external link pointing to a page you are removing, reach out to the linking site and ask them to update the link to point to a relevant active page. Bar associations, legal directories, referral partner websites, and local business associations are usually responsive to a simple email. You built that link equity over years. Spending two hours to retain it is a reasonable trade.

For internal backlinks, update them directly rather than relying on redirects. Redirects work, but a direct link is cleaner. If you have a blog post from three years ago that mentions your now-defunct immigration practice and links to it, update that post to link to something current and relevant. This also gives you a reason to refresh old content, which has its own SEO upside.

---

## Step Four: Update Everything Downstream

After the redirects are in place and the pages are removed, audit everything that referenced the old practice area:

- **Attorney bio pages** — Remove the practice area from every attorney's listed specialties. A bio page that lists a practice area you no longer offer is a credibility problem, not just an SEO problem. It also affects [how potential clients evaluate your firm's authority](/blog/brand-authority-vs-search-authority-law-firms/) when they are comparing you to competitors.
- **Google Business Profile** — Remove the service category if it was listed there.
- **Legal directory listings** (Avvo, Martindale, FindLaw, etc.) — Update every profile. These feed referral traffic and third-party citations that affect local search.
- **Social media bios and service descriptions** — Less critical for SEO, but inconsistency creates friction for anyone doing due diligence on your firm.

Firms that forget this downstream cleanup end up fielding calls about cases they do not take, which wastes intake time and occasionally [muddies the referral relationships](/blog/law-firm-referral-partner-competition/) you have worked to build.

## The Case for Using This Moment to Get Sharper

Here is the overlooked upside of retiring a practice area: it is a forcing function for focus. Firms that try to be everything to everyone dilute their site's topical authority across too many subjects. Google rewards depth and coherence. When you remove a practice area that was never a real business driver, you are not shrinking your SEO footprint — you are tightening it. A tighter, more coherent site tends to rank better for the things you actually want clients calling about.

This is the core argument behind [why boutique specialization compounds SEO rankings over time](/blog/boutique-law-firm-seo-advantage/). Removing the noise is part of building the signal.

The firms that handle **retiring practice area law firm SEO** cleanups well come out with a domain that is healthier, faster to crawl, and more clearly positioned. The firms that just delete pages come out with a mess that takes months to sort out.
