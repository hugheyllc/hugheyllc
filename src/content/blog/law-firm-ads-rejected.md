Why do law firm ads get rejected on Google and Meta? Most of the time the cause is platform policy rather than attorney advertising rules. Google flags misleading claims, unverified advertiser details and landing pages that do not match the ad. Meta flags ad copy that implies it knows something personal about the viewer, and it restricts targeting when a campaign falls into a special ad category.

Bar rules and platform rules are separate systems. An ad can satisfy the state bar and still be disapproved, and an ad can clear a platform review while violating a bar rule. Platform reviewers are largely automated, they do not know what a retainer or a consultation means, and they react to patterns in wording that legal advertising uses constantly.

The practical cost is real. A disapproved ad pauses spend, a repeated problem can put an account under review, and many firms lose days of lead flow before anyone notices. Firms that treat ad policy as a launch checklist item, not a cleanup task, rarely hit these delays.

This guide covers the policy areas that cause most law firm disapprovals and what to change.

## Why Platform Policy Is Not the Same as Bar Compliance

The Florida Bar regulates lawyer advertising under subchapter 4-7, and its [advertising regulation page](https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etad/) explains the rules and filing requirements. Those rules cover things like past results, testimonials and misleading statements. Hughey LLC's guide to [Florida Bar advertising rules](/blog/florida-bar-law-firm-advertising-rules/) walks through them, and federal expectations are covered in the [FTC advertising rules post](/blog/ftc-rules-law-firm-advertising/).

Platforms add a second layer on top. They apply their own policies to wording, claims, landing pages and audience selection, and they apply them to every advertiser at scale. Passing one review does not predict the other.

## What Gets Law Firm Ads Rejected on Google?

Three patterns account for most Google problems: misleading representation, advertiser identity and mismatched destinations.

### Misleading Claims and Missing Qualifications

Google's [misleading representation policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936666) covers ads or landing pages that hide relevant details or misstate information about a business or its services. For law firms, the usual triggers are superlatives and outcome language such as "best lawyers," "guaranteed results" or "winning attorneys," along with claims that a firm is a specialist or board certified without support on the page.

Claims that read as acceptable on a website can still be flagged in a 30-character headline where no context fits. Keep outcome language out of ad text, and put qualifications and disclaimers on the landing page where they can be read in full.

### Advertiser Verification

Google runs an [advertiser verification program](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9703665) that asks for business and identity details, and it can restrict ads while verification is pending. Google's page names financial services as one example of a sector that may be asked to verify and does not single out legal services, so firms should not assume what will or will not be requested. What the page does make clear is that mismatched names, expired documents or inconsistent details are common reasons a verification task fails.

The fix is consistency. The legal name on the billing profile, the website footer, the Google Business Profile and the verification documents should match exactly.

### Landing Page Mismatch

Ads that promise a free case evaluation and send traffic to a page with no form, or that name one practice area and land on a general homepage, draw flags and also waste money. Pair each ad group with a page built for it, as covered in the guide to [law firm landing pages that convert](/blog/law-firm-landing-pages-conversion/).

## How Do Meta's Special Ad Categories Affect Law Firms?

Meta requires advertisers to declare a Special Ad Category when ads relate to credit, employment, housing, or social issues, elections and politics. Its Business Help Center article on [choosing a special ad category](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/298000447747885) explains the process. Declaring a category limits targeting options, which commonly includes narrower age, gender and location controls and restrictions on some audience tools.

Most law firm campaigns do not automatically fall into a category, but some practice areas sit close to the line. Debt relief and bankruptcy touch credit, employment law campaigns touch employment, and immigration or civil rights content can be read as social issues. Meta makes the final call, not the advertiser.

Two rules keep this manageable:

- Check each campaign against the category definitions before launch, not after a rejection.
- Never skip a required declaration to keep targeting options. Firms that do this risk disapprovals and account restrictions.

## Meta Copy Problems: The Personal Attributes Trap

Meta also reviews wording. Its personal attributes standard targets ad copy that asserts or implies knowledge of a person's characteristics, including health, finances and criminal history. Legal ads fall into this constantly because they speak to a problem: "Facing a DUI charge?" or "Drowning in debt?" can be read as addressing the viewer's situation directly.

Rewrite toward the service rather than the viewer. "DUI defense for Tampa drivers" and "Debt relief options explained by an attorney" describe what the firm offers without implying anything about the person seeing it. Test several versions, since the same idea can pass in one phrasing and fail in another.

## What to Do When a Law Firm Ad Is Disapproved

A clean process keeps one disapproval from becoming an account problem.

1. Read the exact policy named in the rejection notice before changing anything.
2. Edit the specific text or page that triggered it, and resubmit instead of recreating the ad.
3. Appeal only when the ad clearly complies, with a short explanation and no repeated submissions.
4. Keep a log of rejections by policy so patterns show up in copy templates.
5. Review [account structure and negative keywords](/blog/law-firm-negative-keywords/) at the same time, because off-topic traffic often brings the scrutiny first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do Google Ads for lawyers keep getting disapproved?

The common causes are outcome or superlative claims in ad text, mismatches between ad and landing page, and business details that do not match across the account and website. The disapproval notice names the specific policy, which is the starting point for the fix.

### Do law firm ads on Facebook need a special ad category?

Not automatically. The requirement depends on what the ad is about, such as credit, employment, housing or social issues. Debt-related and employment-related practice areas are the most likely to be affected. Meta decides, so review the category definitions before launching each campaign.

### Can a law firm appeal a rejected ad?

Yes. Both platforms offer an appeal or re-review option. Appeals work best when the ad clearly complies and the explanation is brief. If the wording is borderline, editing the ad and resubmitting is usually faster than arguing.

### Does passing platform review mean an ad is compliant with bar rules?

No. Platform approval says nothing about state bar compliance. Firms remain responsible for meeting their jurisdiction's attorney advertising rules, including any filing requirements. This is not legal advice, so confirm the specifics with the state bar.
