How do you measure law firm TV advertising when no one clicks? You stop looking for click-level proof and measure at the market level instead. The reliable signals are changes in branded search, changes in total inquiries in the markets where the ads ran, and a consistent way of recording how each caller says they found the firm.

No single number will settle it. Television, radio and billboards work by building familiarity before someone needs a lawyer, so the effect shows up as a pattern across several data sources rather than as a line in an ad platform report. Firms that treat these channels like paid search, and then judge them by cost per click or last-touch conversions, almost always conclude the spend is wasted or, just as wrongly, that it is working because it feels premium.

This post covers which numbers mislead, how to read market-level lift, how to use branded search as a signal, and how to code intake sources so the data exists when a renewal decision arrives.

## Why Vanity Numbers Mislead on Offline Media

Impressions, reach, gross rating points and "daily vehicle counts" describe how many people could have seen an ad. They do not describe how many people acted. Stations and outdoor vendors report them because they are easy to produce, and they will almost always look impressive.

A second trap is the website analytics report. A viewer who sees a billboard on the way to work and searches the firm's name three weeks later is recorded as organic or direct traffic. The channel that created the demand gets no credit, and the channel that captured it looks better than it is. The [brand and performance budget allocation problem](/blog/law-firm-brand-performance-marketing-budget-allocation/) starts exactly here.

Treat vendor reports as a description of exposure only. Everything about results has to come from the firm's own data.

## How Do You Measure Law Firm TV Advertising at the Market Level?

Market-level lift compares what happened where the ads ran against what would have happened without them. The cleanest approach is a geographic test.

1. Pick a baseline period, ideally 8 to 12 weeks before launch, and record weekly inquiries, signed cases and branded search volume.
2. Choose a test market and, if the firm operates in more than one, a similar control market that receives no offline advertising.
3. Run the campaign long enough to cover several intake cycles, then keep measuring for weeks after it ends, because demand pulled forward by the campaign can show up as a dip later.
4. Compare the change in the test market with the change in the control market, not with the prior month.

Single-market firms cannot run a true control, but they can still use time-based comparisons. Compare the flight weeks with the same weeks in prior years, and note anything else that changed, such as a new paid search budget or a seasonal spike. Splitting regions to isolate an effect is the same logic ad platforms use for their own lift studies.

Decide before launch what result would justify renewing. A rule written after the data arrives tends to favor whatever the firm already wanted to do.

## What Is Branded Search Lift and How Do You Read It?

Branded search lift is the increase in people searching for the firm by name, an attorney's name or a campaign phrase after exposure to offline advertising. It is the closest thing offline media has to a click, because a person who sees a billboard or hears a radio spot often remembers the name and types it later.

Pull branded query data from Google's [Search Console performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start) and compare weekly impressions for the firm name before, during and after the flight. If the firm bids on its own name in paid search, track those impressions too. Be careful, though: a spike in branded searches can also come from a news story, a referral wave or a seasonal pattern, so compare against a control period or market where possible.

Branded search lift is also where [branded keyword bidding waste](/blog/law-firms-branded-keyword-bidding-waste/) gets expensive. If offline media drives people to search the firm's name and a competitor bids on that name, the investment leaks to someone else.

Useful secondary signals include direct traffic, calls to the main number and any short vanity URL or phone number used only in the ad. Each one is imperfect, and together they are persuasive.

## How Should Intake Teams Code the Source of Every Lead?

Intake source coding is the habit that makes everything above checkable. Every new inquiry gets one source recorded by the person answering, and the list of options must be specific enough to be useful.

- A single field in the CRM with a fixed list: TV, radio, billboard, referral, search, social, directory, other.
- A required follow-up for "other" so it does not become a dumping ground.
- A prompt that asks an open question, such as "How did you first hear about the firm?" rather than "Did you see the firm's ad?"
- Separate call tracking numbers for each offline channel, so the call log provides a second record.

Self-reported answers are imperfect. Callers forget, mix up channels or name the last thing they did, such as Google. That is acceptable. The goal is directional consistency over months, and a source field that shifts toward "TV" during a flight is meaningful even if the exact count is off. The [call tracking and CRM connection guide](/blog/call-tracking-crm-web-analytics-how-to-connect-the-dots-for-your-firms-intake/) covers how to connect those records to cases, and a regular [intake process audit](/blog/law-firm-intake-process-audit/) keeps the coding honest.

Track signed cases and case value by source, not only inquiries. Offline media often produces a high volume of low-fit calls, so cost per inquiry can look fine while [cost per retained client](/blog/cost-per-retained-client-law-firm/) tells a different story.

## What Compliance Rules Apply to Broadcast and Outdoor Ads?

Measurement is not the only constraint. Television, radio and billboard ads are still lawyer advertising, and Florida lawyers must follow the advertising provisions in the [Rules Regulating The Florida Bar](https://www.floridabar.org/rules/rrtfb/), including required disclosures, limits on misleading claims and rules on testimonials and results. The Bar's [advertising regulation page](https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etad/) explains the filing and review process. Firms in other states should check their own bar's rules before a campaign goes to production. This is not legal advice.

Short-format media makes compliance harder, since a 15-second spot has little room for disclaimers. Build the compliance review into the production schedule, not the end of it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is TV advertising worth it for a law firm?

It can be, but only when the firm has the budget for sustained frequency, a market large enough to justify the cost and a way to measure results. Short flights with no tracking rarely produce usable evidence. Firms should set a renewal threshold before launching and judge the campaign against it.

### How do you track calls from a billboard?

Use a dedicated tracking number or vanity URL on the billboard, and train intake staff to ask how each caller first heard of the firm. Neither method captures everyone, since many people search the firm name later, so also watch branded search volume during the campaign period.

### How long does radio advertising take to show results?

Radio typically needs repeated exposure over several weeks before listeners act, so a two-week test is rarely enough. Measure for the full flight and for several weeks afterward, and compare branded search and inquiry volume against a baseline period before judging the result.

### Should a law firm cut paid search to fund offline advertising?

Usually not. Offline media tends to create demand that paid search and organic listings then capture, so cutting search can reduce the return from both. Test offline spending as an addition or a reallocation of a small share, and watch total signed cases rather than channel reports.
