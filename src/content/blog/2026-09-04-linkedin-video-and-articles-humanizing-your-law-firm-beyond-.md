LinkedIn video and articles give law firms a way to show the people behind the practice — not just the credentials on the wall. In my experience, the firms that grow the fastest on LinkedIn aren't the ones posting the most frequently; they're the ones making potential clients feel something when they scroll past a piece of content. Text posts have their place, but video and long-form articles create a dimension of trust that a 200-word status update simply cannot replicate. When someone watches you speak on camera for two minutes, they've essentially met you. They've heard your voice, seen how you think, and formed an impression that would otherwise take a phone call or a face-to-face meeting to establish. That's a remarkable shortcut in a business where relationships drive everything. And long-form LinkedIn articles? They signal depth. They tell prospective clients and referral partners that you're not just recycling hot takes — you're actually working through complex ideas in public, which is exactly what sophisticated clients want to see from the lawyers they're considering hiring.

## Why Text Posts Alone Leave a Trust Gap

Most law firms start their LinkedIn presence the same way: a few text posts about a recent case win, a firm announcement, maybe a congratulations to a new associate. It's a reasonable beginning, but it creates a ceiling. Text posts on LinkedIn tend to perform well in terms of impressions, but impressions don't automatically convert to relationships. From what I've seen working with firms across practice areas, the engagement that actually moves the needle — the DM from a potential client, the referral from a financial advisor who's been watching your content — almost always traces back to something more personal than a text update. There's a trust gap between "I've seen this firm's name" and "I feel like I know these people well enough to call them." Video and articles are the bridge.

## What LinkedIn Video Actually Does for a Law Firm

I want to be direct about something: most lawyers are afraid of video, and most of those fears are unfounded. You don't need a production crew. You don't need a teleprompter. In my experience, the videos that perform best for law firms are the ones that feel like a conversation, not a commercial. A partner sitting at their desk explaining what happens during a business acquisition, or a family law attorney walking through what to expect in a mediation session — these simple, practical videos build enormous credibility over time.

LinkedIn's native video also gets preferential treatment in the algorithm, which means your reach extends well beyond your existing connections. I've found that firms who commit to even one or two short videos per month see a meaningful increase in profile views from people they've never interacted with before. The key is consistency and relevance, not production value. Speak to a specific problem your ideal client is trying to solve, and keep it under three minutes.

## Long-Form Articles: The Underused Powerhouse

LinkedIn articles are one of the most underutilized tools in law firm marketing, and I genuinely don't understand why more firms aren't using them. Unlike text posts, articles are indexed by search engines and live permanently on your profile. They position you as a thought leader in a way that a quick post simply cannot. A well-written article on, say, the estate planning implications of a recent tax change, or what business owners need to know about non-compete law in your state, tells readers something important: this person has done the thinking, not just the tweeting.

As I explored in [Thought Leadership on LinkedIn for Lawyers](/blog/thought-leadership-on-linkedin-for-lawyers/), the firms that use the platform most effectively treat it as a publishing channel, not just a networking tool. That mindset shift changes everything about how you approach content. Instead of asking "what should I post today," you start asking "what do I know that would genuinely help my target audience make a better decision?" That's the question that leads to article-worthy content.

## Combining Video and Articles Into a Content Rhythm

Here's a practical approach I recommend to many of the firms I work with: pair a short video with a longer article on the same topic. Record a two-minute video explaining the key points of a legal issue, then write a 600-900 word article that goes deeper. Post the video first to generate attention, then follow up a few days later with the article for the people who want more detail. This approach serves different kinds of learners and creates multiple touchpoints around a single idea.

This also integrates naturally with a broader content strategy. If you've already built out a blog on your firm's website, LinkedIn articles can serve as a companion channel — adapting existing content for a professional audience rather than starting from scratch. I've talked about the importance of a coordinated content approach in pieces like [Law Firm Blog Strategy](/blog/law-firm-blog-strategy-content-that-converts/), because the firms that gain the most traction are almost never doing one thing in isolation. Everything works better when it reinforces everything else.

## The Human Element Is the Strategy

There's a temptation in law firm marketing to keep everything at arm's length — professional, polished, and impersonal. I understand where that instinct comes from. Law is a serious business, and firms worry about appearing too casual or saying something they shouldn't. But in my experience, the biggest mistake most law firms make on LinkedIn isn't saying too much. It's saying nothing real at all.

Clients hire lawyers they trust, and they trust people they feel they know. Video and articles are simply the most efficient tools LinkedIn offers for creating that sense of knowing someone. A managing partner who posts a short video explaining their philosophy on client communication is doing more for new business development than a dozen text posts about the firm's latest accolade. Authenticity isn't a risk — it's the whole point.

This connects to something I've written about in [Personal Branding for Lawyers on LinkedIn](/blog/personal-branding-for-lawyers-on-linkedin/): your individual attorneys are your firm's most powerful marketing assets. Their voices, personalities, and expertise are what differentiate you from every other firm with a similar practice area and similar credentials.

## Getting Started Without Overthinking It

If you're not currently using video or articles on LinkedIn, start with one video this week. Pick a question you get asked regularly, record a straight-to-camera answer on your phone, and post it with a two or three sentence introduction. Don't edit it to death. Watch what happens to your engagement.

Then commit to one LinkedIn article per month on a topic that genuinely matters to your target clients. Over time, you'll build a library of content that works for your firm long after you've moved on to the next piece.
