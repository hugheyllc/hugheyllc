# PPC vs. Organic: The Blended Cost Analysis Law Firms Never Run

Should a law firm invest more in PPC or organic SEO? Wrong question. The right question is: what does a lead actually cost when both channels are running simultaneously — and how does each one change the other's performance? **Law firm paid search vs organic cost** isn't a comparison you make in isolation. It's a system. Firms that treat it like two separate budget lines are measuring the wrong thing and making budget decisions based on incomplete data. The real cost analysis — the one that should be driving your marketing spend — is a blended view that accounts for how paid and organic traffic interact, overlap, and amplify each other.

Most managing partners never run this analysis. Not because it's complicated, but because nobody builds the model for them. This post does that.

---

## Why the Separated View Lies to You

When your marketing team (or agency) reports PPC performance separately from SEO performance, you get two sets of numbers that look clean and logical. PPC shows you cost-per-click, cost-per-lead, and conversion rate. SEO shows you traffic volume, keyword rankings, and leads attributed to organic. Both look defensible.

The problem: **both channels are taking credit for the same leads**.

A prospective client searches for a personal injury attorney in Tampa. They see your Google ad, don't click it. Three days later, they search again, find your blog post ranking organically, read it, and fill out a contact form. Your PPC dashboard shows an impression. Your SEO dashboard shows a conversion. Neither tells you the full story.

This isn't a tracking failure. It's a structural problem with how law firms frame the analysis. The channels don't operate independently — they operate as a system — and your cost-per-lead calculation needs to reflect that.

---

## What a Blended Cost Analysis Actually Looks Like

A proper blended analysis starts with total marketing spend across both channels and divides it by total qualified leads — not channel-attributed leads. Simple in concept. Rare in practice.

**Step one: aggregate spend.** Add your monthly PPC budget (including agency management fees) to your monthly SEO spend (content, technical work, link building, agency fees). That's your denominator.

**Step two: consolidate leads.** Pull every qualified inquiry for the same period. Not channel-attributed leads. Total leads. If your intake team logged 40 inquiries this month, that's your numerator.

**Step three: calculate blended CPL.** Total spend divided by total leads. That's your true cost per lead.

**Step four: segment by matter type.** A blended CPL of $400 means nothing without context. A $400 lead for an estate planning consultation and a $400 lead for a commercial litigation matter are not the same thing. Segment by practice area. Now you have something you can act on.

This connects directly to understanding [when your marketing spend stops making sense](/blog/client-acquisition-cost-threshold-law-firms/) — because blended CPL is the number that actually tells you whether you're over the threshold.

## The Organic Multiplier Effect on Paid Search

Here's where it gets interesting. Law firms that run PPC alongside a strong organic presence consistently see lower cost-per-click on paid. Why? **Quality Score.**

Google's Quality Score rewards relevance — and a law firm website with deep, well-structured content on a practice area signals relevance more convincingly than a thin landing page. Your organic investment directly subsidizes your paid search efficiency. When you only look at **law firm paid search vs organic cost** as separate line items, you miss this relationship entirely.

The reverse is also true. Paid search creates brand familiarity. Someone who saw your ad twice before finding you organically is more likely to convert when they do land on your site. Your paid impressions are warming up organic traffic that you'll never attribute to the paid campaign. That's invisible ROI — but it's real.

If your organic traffic has plateaued despite ongoing SEO investment, this interaction effect is worth examining closely. The issue may not be your organic strategy in isolation — it might be what's missing from [the broader system driving your traffic ceiling](/blog/law-firm-seo-plateau-organic-traffic-ceiling/).

## How Competition Level Changes the Math

High-competition markets — personal injury in major metros, mass tort, criminal defense in urban markets — have PPC costs that are genuinely difficult to justify on a pure paid basis. Cost-per-click in these categories can run into the hundreds of dollars. Cost-per-lead, even higher.

In those markets, **organic search carries more of the weight** over time, and the economics of building organic authority start to look very different. The upfront investment in content and technical SEO is amortized across months and years of traffic. A page ranking well for a high-intent keyword delivers leads at a declining marginal cost as time goes on. PPC delivers leads at a flat or increasing marginal cost as competition intensifies.

But here's what the math also shows: **in high-competition markets, going organic-only is slow and painful.** PPC buys you leads while organic authority builds. The blended model isn't optional — it's the only practical path. The question is what ratio makes sense, and that ratio shifts depending on your market, your current organic position, and your tolerance for a long investment runway.

This is the same logic behind [how law firms should split budgets between brand and performance channels](/blog/law-firm-brand-performance-marketing-budget-allocation/) — the allocation is never static, and it's never the same for two different firms.

## The Data You're Probably Missing

Running this analysis requires data that most law firms don't have clean access to. Intake data lives in a CRM. Marketing data lives in Google Ads and Google Analytics. They don't talk to each other without deliberate integration work.

If your intake process doesn't capture how a prospective client first heard about your firm — and how they found you immediately before calling — you're flying blind on the blended model. You can't calculate a true blended CPL if you can't attribute leads to the period in which they were acquired.

Worth noting: your CRM might be causing more damage than you realize. Beyond attribution gaps, [the way client intake data is structured in your CRM can actively suppress your organic rankings](/blog/crm-seo-sabotage-law-firms/) — which means your organic cost appears lower than it is because your organic performance is being degraded without anyone noticing.

Also worth auditing: if your website is producing low-quality content at volume, it's not just wasting budget. [It's actively harming the organic performance you're depending on to make the blended model work](/blog/law-firm-blog-killing-rankings/).

---

## What Changes When You Run the Blended Analysis

Firms that run this model for the first time usually discover two things:

First, their **pure PPC leads cost less than they thought** once you account for the brand lift and Quality Score benefits from organic. The channel isn't as inefficient as the standalone numbers suggest.

Second, their **organic leads cost more than they thought** once you fully load in all SEO-related spend — including content production, technical maintenance, and the time cost of managing it. The channel isn't as cheap as the standalone numbers suggest.

The blended number is more honest than either channel number alone. And once you have an honest number, you can make an honest budget decision.
