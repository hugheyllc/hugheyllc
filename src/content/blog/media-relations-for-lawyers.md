How do lawyers get quoted in news stories? They become the dependable source a reporter can reach on deadline, with a clear opinion about something happening in the reporter's coverage area. Media relations for lawyers is less about pitching your firm and more about supplying what journalists are short on: fast, accurate, plain-language explanations of legal events.

A reporter writing about a new state law, a large verdict or a changing court rule needs one or two people who can say what it means for readers. The lawyers who get called are the ones who answered quickly last time, did not hedge every sentence and did not turn the interview into an advertisement.

The payoff is real but indirect. A quote in a local newspaper, trade outlet or broadcast segment builds recognition, supports the credibility signals covered in [E-E-A-T for law firm SEO](/blog/eeat-law-firm-seo/), and sometimes produces a link back to the firm's site. It rarely produces a flood of calls. Treat it as a long-term authority program, not a lead source you can forecast.

## What Do Reporters Want From Media Relations for Lawyers?

Reporters on deadline need three things: speed, clarity and a point of view. Roundups of media advice for lawyers consistently say that a fast reply often decides who gets quoted, and that hedged answers get cut. A comment that explains the issue in two plain sentences gets used. A paragraph of qualifications does not.

They also want relevance to their beat. A legal trade reporter wants analysis of what a ruling means for the market. A business reporter wants a lawyer's read on how a new rule affects local employers. A consumer reporter wants to know what readers should do. Pitching the wrong angle to the wrong reporter wastes both sides' time.

What reporters do not want is promotion. A pitch that reads like a press release for the firm gets deleted.

## How Do You Find and Pitch the Right Reporters?

Start narrow. Build a list of 10 to 20 reporters and editors who cover topics connected to your practice area in your market: local news, business journals, legal trade publications and regional broadcast. Read their last several articles before contacting anyone.

A workable pitch is short:

- A subject line that names the news hook, such as a new statute, a pending decision or a deadline.
- Two or three sentences stating what is happening and what your view is.
- One line on why you are qualified to comment.
- Your direct phone number and a promise of availability the same day.

Pitch to events, not to yourself. "The new Florida law takes effect July 1 and changes what employers must disclose" gives a reporter a reason to respond. "The firm has 20 years of experience" does not.

Between pitches, build familiarity. Follow reporters on LinkedIn, share their work with a useful comment and send a short note when a story prompts a relevant observation. The link-earning side of this is covered in the broader [law firm link-building strategy](/blog/law-firm-link-building-strategy/), but the relationship work is what keeps reporters returning.

## What Are the Ethics Rules for Talking to the Press?

Talking to reporters is not advertising in the usual sense, but ethics rules still apply. Two areas matter most.

First, confidentiality. Information relating to a client representation is protected under Model Rule 1.6 and its state equivalents, even when it is publicly available elsewhere. Do not discuss a current client's matter without informed consent.

Second, pending cases. [ABA Model Rule 3.6 on trial publicity](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_3_6_trial_publicity/) limits statements a lawyer involved in a matter should reasonably expect to materially prejudice the proceeding. States adopt their own versions, so check yours. The ABA Journal has a useful summary of [ethical pitfalls when speaking with the media](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/journal/articles/2022/watch-out-for-these-ethical-constraints-and-pitfalls-when-speaki/).

Interviews can also become advertising problems if the firm later reuses them. Once a quote appears in a firm's marketing, it falls under the same standards covered in [Florida Bar advertising rules for law firms](/blog/florida-bar-law-firm-advertising-rules/), including limits on implying guaranteed outcomes. This is not legal advice, and firms should confirm requirements with their own bar.

## How Do You Turn a Quote Into Long-Term Value?

A single quote only helps if the firm captures it. Firms that get consistent value from press do a few things after publication:

1. Add the placement to a dedicated "In the News" page, with the outlet name and a link.
2. Reference the coverage on relevant attorney bio pages and practice area pages.
3. Share it on LinkedIn with context about what the story means for readers.
4. Keep a running list of reporters who responded, with their beats and preferred contact method.

Search value comes from the link and the brand mention, but only when the link comes from an editorial decision. Paid placements and link schemes are a different matter, and Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) address link spam directly. Earned coverage is the safe version of the same goal.

Press coverage also feeds the reputation side of marketing. See [law firm reputation management](/blog/law-firm-reputation-management/) for how news mentions fit alongside reviews and directory profiles.

## Measuring Whether Media Relations Is Working

Count placements, but also watch downstream signals: branded search volume, referring domains from news sites, direct traffic to attorney bio pages and the number of new clients who mention seeing the firm in the news. Ask on intake calls. Most firms never do, so they cannot tell whether press coverage matters to them.

Expect slow results. A reasonable first goal is a handful of relationships with reporters who know the firm answers quickly. Placements follow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do lawyers need a PR firm to get media coverage?

Not necessarily. A single attorney who responds quickly, understands a reporter's beat and offers clear commentary can earn coverage without an agency. A PR firm helps when the firm lacks time to maintain relationships or needs help with crisis communication or high-profile matters.

### How many reporters should a law firm build relationships with?

Start with 10 to 20 reporters whose beats overlap the firm's practice areas. A small, carefully researched list produces better results than a mass email to hundreds of journalists, because reporters can tell when a pitch was not written for them.

### Can a lawyer comment on a client's case in the news?

Only with care. Client confidentiality rules protect information about a representation, and trial publicity rules limit statements about pending matters. Get informed client consent first, keep comments to public record facts, and check the version of the rules in your state.

### Does being quoted in the news help law firm SEO?

It can. Editorial links from news sites carry credibility, and brand mentions support authority signals. The effect varies by outlet and link type, so treat SEO as one benefit alongside recognition and referrals rather than a guaranteed result.
